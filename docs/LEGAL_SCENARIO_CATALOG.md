## SCEN_PROP_001: Resolving ownership dispute involving GPA and unregistered sale agreement

* **Domain:** `Property & Real Estate` | **Subdomain:** `Title Verification & Conveyancing`
* **Jurisdiction:** State of Karnataka (Civil Court of competent territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction)
* **Competent Authority / Forum:** **Senior Civil Judge / Principal Civil Judge (Bengaluru Urban / Rural)**
* **Applicable Statutes:** `Transfer of Property Act, 1882; Registration Act, 1908; Specific Relief Act, 1963` (TPA Section 54, 53A; Registration Act Section 17, 49; SRA Section 5, 34)
* **Required Factual Context:** Purchased property through General Power of Attorney and notarized agreement to sell in Bengaluru; seller subsequently executed registered sale deed to third party; buyer has physical possession but no registered deed.
* **Statutory Exceptions / Caveats:** Doctrine of part performance under Section 53A protects possession as shield only if agreement was registered and stamp duty paid; does not create ownership title.
* **Available Remedies:** Suit for specific performance of contract (if within 3-year limitation) or suit for recovery of money with damages; defense of protected possession under 53A against third-party buyer.
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Conduct 30-year Encumbrance Certificate search on Kaveri 2.0. 2. Verify registered documents. 3. Issue legal notice to seller demanding execution of registered conveyance. 4. Institute civil suit for specific performance and interim temporary injunction under Order XXXIX CPC.
* **Mandatory Documents:** Original agreement to sell, GPA document, payment receipts / bank bank statements, electricity/water bills showing physical possession, 30-year EC extract from Kaveri 2.0.
* **Statutory Deadlines / Limitation:** **Specific performance: 3 years from date fixed for performance or from date buyer has notice of refusal (Article 54 Limitation Act).**
* **Controlling Precedents:** Suraj Lamp (2012) 1 SCC 656 (GPA sales do not convey title); Anathula Sudhakar (2008) 4 SCC 594 (suit for declaration necessary when title clouded).

## SCEN_PROP_002: Rectifying mutation rejection and converting B-Khata to A-Khata in Bengaluru

* **Domain:** `Property & Land Governance` | **Subdomain:** `Karnataka Land Revenue & Khata`
* **Jurisdiction:** Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) / Bengaluru Urban Revenue Division
* **Competent Authority / Forum:** **Assistant Revenue Officer (BBMP) for Khata; Tahsildar / Assistant Commissioner for Land Revenue Mutation**
* **Applicable Statutes:** `Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike Act, 2020; Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964` (BBMP Act Section 144, 313; KLR Act Section 95, 128)
* **Required Factual Context:** Buyer purchased a site in unapproved layout in Bengaluru Urban; currently holds B-Khata tax receipt; wishes to secure building plan sanction and regularize mutation.
* **Statutory Exceptions / Caveats:** B-Khata cannot be converted to A-Khata without valid agricultural land conversion under Section 95 KLR Act and layout approval from BDA/BMRDA.
* **Available Remedies:** Apply for Section 95 land conversion; pay betterment and statutory development charges to BBMP once regularization scheme is notified; challenge wrongful mutation rejection before Assistant Commissioner.
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Verify parent land conversion order and survey sketch (Tippani). 2. Submit mutation application on Bhoomi portal. 3. Upon conversion and DC approval, apply for BBMP A-Khata bifurcation with conversion order and title deeds.
* **Mandatory Documents:** Registered Sale Deed, Parent title documents (30 years), Bhoomi RTC, Conversion order under Section 95, Survey sketch, Tax paid receipts, Aadhaar/PAN.
* **Statutory Deadlines / Limitation:** **Bhoomi mutation: 30 days under Sakala Services Act; Revenue Appeal under Section 136(2): 60 days from mutation rejection order.**
* **Controlling Precedents:** Jitendra Singh (2021) SCC OnLine SC 802 (Khata/mutation is not title); State of Karnataka v. Shankaraiah (High Court of Karnataka).

## SCEN_CRIM_001: Securing anticipatory bail and challenging threatened arrest under BNS 318/316

* **Domain:** `Criminal Law & Procedure` | **Subdomain:** `Arrest, Bail & FIR Registration`
* **Jurisdiction:** Sessions Court / High Court of Karnataka
* **Competent Authority / Forum:** **Principal City Civil and Sessions Judge / High Court of Karnataka**
* **Applicable Statutes:** `Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023; Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023` (BNSS Section 35, 173, 482; BNS Section 316, 318)
* **Required Factual Context:** Business partner filed a police complaint alleging cheating and criminal breach of trust regarding commercial invoice default; police visiting residence and threatening arrest without notice.
* **Statutory Exceptions / Caveats:** Pure contractual/civil disputes cannot be converted into criminal cheating unless fraudulent intention existed at the inception of the contract.
* **Available Remedies:** File Anticipatory Bail application under Section 482 BNSS; seek notice under Section 35(3) BNSS; file Section 528 BNSS (482 CrPC) petition to quash FIR.
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Obtain copy of FIR from police station or portal. 2. Draft Anticipatory Bail petition establishing civil/commercial nature of transaction. 3. Move urgent motion before Sessions Court for interim anticipatory protection.
* **Mandatory Documents:** Copy of FIR/complaint, commercial agreements, invoices, ledger statements, proof of communication, ID and address proof.
* **Statutory Deadlines / Limitation:** **Anticipatory bail: prior to arrest; Section 35(3) notice response: within stipulated notice period.**
* **Controlling Precedents:** Arnesh Kumar (2014) 8 SCC 273 (no automatic arrest for <7 yr offences); Satender Kumar Antil (2022) 10 SCC 51 (bail as rule); Siddharth (2022) 1 SCC 676 (no arrest required for chargesheet).

## SCEN_BANK_001: Filing statutory complaint for dishonoured cheque of Rs 15 lakh

* **Domain:** `Banking & Commercial Law` | **Subdomain:** `Cheque Bounce (Section 138 NI Act)`
* **Jurisdiction:** Judicial Magistrate First Class / Metropolitan Magistrate (where payee bank account is maintained)
* **Competent Authority / Forum:** **Court of Small Causes / Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC)**
* **Applicable Statutes:** `Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881; Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023` (NI Act Section 138, 139, 142, 143A; BNSS Section 223)
* **Required Factual Context:** Business cheque issued for goods delivered returned unpaid with bank memo 'Funds Insufficient'; payee received memo 10 days ago.
* **Statutory Exceptions / Caveats:** Notice dispatched after 30 days of bank memo invalidates Section 138 complaint; security cheques for non-existent debt rebuttable under Section 139.
* **Available Remedies:** Criminal prosecution under Section 138 (imprisonment up to 2 years, fine up to twice cheque amount); 20% interim compensation under Section 143A.
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Dispatch formal legal demand notice via registered speed post within 30 days of memo. 2. Wait 15 days for payment window to expire. 3. File criminal complaint under Section 142 within 30 days of expiry before JMFC. 4. Tender pre-summoning affidavit.
* **Mandatory Documents:** Original dishonoured cheque, bank return memo, copy of statutory demand notice, postal speed post receipt, delivery tracking report, underlying tax invoices.
* **Statutory Deadlines / Limitation:** **Demand notice: 30 days from bank memo; Payment window: 15 days from notice delivery; Court complaint: 30 days after payment window.**
* **Controlling Precedents:** Rangappa v. Sri Mohan (2010) 11 SCC 441 (presumption of legally enforceable debt); Dashrath Rupsingh Rathod v. State of Maharashtra (payee bank jurisdiction).

## SCEN_FAM_001: Claiming emergency protection, residence order, and monthly maintenance under PWDVA

* **Domain:** `Family & Matrimonial Law` | **Subdomain:** `Domestic Violence & Maintenance`
* **Jurisdiction:** Metropolitan Magistrate / JMFC (where aggrieved woman resides or incident occurred)
* **Competent Authority / Forum:** **Chief Metropolitan Magistrate (Bengaluru) / Judicial Magistrate First Class**
* **Applicable Statutes:** `Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005; Hindu Marriage Act, 1955; BNSS, 2023` (PWDVA Section 3, 12, 18, 19, 20, 23; BNSS Section 144)
* **Required Factual Context:** Wife subjected to physical and economic harassment by husband and mother-in-law for additional dowry; husband locked her out of rented shared flat in Bengaluru.
* **Statutory Exceptions / Caveats:** Husband claiming flat is rented in mother's name; settled under Hiral Harsora that mother-in-law is respondent, and shared household protection applies regardless of who holds tenancy.
* **Available Remedies:** Interim ex-parte protection order (Section 18); Residence order restraining dispossession (Section 19); Monetary maintenance for self and child (Section 20); Compensation (Section 22).
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Approach Protection Officer (WCD Dept) to record Domestic Incident Report (DIR). 2. File Section 12 application before Magistrate with Section 23 interim prayer. 3. File Rajnesh v. Neha Affidavit of Assets and Liabilities.
* **Mandatory Documents:** Marriage certificate/photos, DIR from Protection Officer, medical records of injury, bank statements, Rajnesh v. Neha asset disclosure affidavit.
* **Statutory Deadlines / Limitation:** **60-day target for disposal of interim application under Section 12(5) PWDVA.**
* **Controlling Precedents:** Rajnesh v. Neha (2021) 2 SCC 324 (mandatory asset affidavit); Hiral P. Harsora (2016) 10 SCC 165 (women respondents covered); Prabha Tyagi (2022) (subsisting shared household right).

## SCEN_CONS_001: Claiming full refund with interest from builder for 3-year project delay in Bengaluru

* **Domain:** `Consumer Protection` | **Subdomain:** `Real Estate & Builder Delay`
* **Jurisdiction:** Karnataka State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (Bangalore) / Karnataka RERA
* **Competent Authority / Forum:** **State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Karnataka / Adjudicating Officer, Karnataka RERA**
* **Applicable Statutes:** `Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016` (CPA Section 35, 47; RERA Section 18, 31)
* **Required Factual Context:** Allottee paid 90% consideration (Rs 75 lakh) for flat in Bengaluru; builder promised possession in December 2021; project incomplete with no Occupancy Certificate in 2024.
* **Statutory Exceptions / Caveats:** Builder citing force majeure (COVID-19); Supreme Court held COVID extensions apply only for specified statutory periods, not for indefinite multi-year delays.
* **Available Remedies:** Full refund of deposited consideration with interest (SBI MCLR + 2%) or monthly delay compensation until actual handover of possession with valid OC.
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Issue formal demand notice to builder. 2. File consumer complaint online on e-Daakhil portal or RERA complaint on rera.karnataka.gov.in. 3. Claim refund with statutory interest and mental agony damages.
* **Mandatory Documents:** Builder-Buyer Agreement, payment receipts, bank loan disbursement statements, email correspondence, brochure/sanctioned plan copy.
* **Statutory Deadlines / Limitation:** **2 years from promised possession date or ongoing cause of action (Section 69 CPA).**
* **Controlling Precedents:** Imperia Structures (2020) 10 SCC 783 (concurrent consumer remedy); Pioneer Urban (2019) 5 SCC 725 (one-sided builder agreement unfair trade practice); Newtech Promoters (2021) (retroactive application).
