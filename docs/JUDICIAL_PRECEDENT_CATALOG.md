## Suraj Lamp & Industries Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Haryana — `(2012) 1 SCC 656 / 2011 INSC 752`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 3-Judge Bench (R.V. Raveendran, A.K. Patnaik, H.L. Gokhale, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2011-10-11 | **Case Number:** Special Leave Petition (C) No. 13917 of 2009
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MTI0NjI=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MTI0NjI=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `Transfer of Property Act 1882 (Sec 54), Registration Act 1908 (Sec 17, 49), Powers-of-Attorney Act 1882 (Sec 2)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 12, 16, 18, 19, 22
* **Material Facts:** Widespread practice of transferring immovable property using General Power of Attorney (GPA), Agreement to Sell (SA), and Living Wills (SA/GPA/WILL transfers) to evade stamp duty and capital gains.
* **Legal Issue:** Whether an immovable property can be lawfully transferred or ownership conveyed through an Agreement to Sell, GPA, or Will?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** A power of attorney is an agency creation and does not convey title. Under Section 54 of Transfer of Property Act, 1882, transfer of immovable property can only be effected by a registered conveyance deed.
* **Holding:** **SA/GPA/WILL transfers do not convey title or create ownership interest in immovable property. Courts will not recognize such transactions as completed sales.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Followed and affirmed in Ghanshyam v. Yogendra Rathi (2023) and Shakeel Ahmed v. Syed Akhlaq Hussain (2023).
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma — `(2020) 9 SCC 1 / 2020 INSC 483`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 3-Judge Bench (Arun Mishra, S. Abdul Nazeer, M.R. Shah, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2020-08-11 | **Case Number:** Civil Appeal No. 32601 of 2018
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzA4NzI=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzA4NzI=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (Section 6, as amended by Act 39 of 2005)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 60, 68, 75, 129, 137
* **Material Facts:** Conflicting High Court and Supreme Court interpretations on whether a daughter acquires coparcenary rights under Section 6 HSA 2005 amendment if her father had died before September 9, 2005.
* **Legal Issue:** Whether the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 confers coparcenary rights on daughters by birth retrospectively/retroactively regardless of father death date?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** The right is conferred by birth and is not dependent on the father living at the time of the 2005 amendment. Daughters have equal coparcenary rights from birth.
* **Holding:** **Daughters have coparcenary rights in Mitakshara coparcenary property by birth in the same manner as sons, irrespective of whether the father was alive on September 9, 2005.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Settled law; confirmed in multiple Supreme Court and High Court partition rulings.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Rajnesh v. Neha — `(2021) 2 SCC 324 / 2020 INSC 629`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 2-Judge Bench (Indu Malhotra, R. Subhash Reddy, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2020-11-04 | **Case Number:** Criminal Appeal No. 730 of 2020
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzEzNjU=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzEzNjU=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `BNSS 2023 (Sec 144) / CrPC 1973 (Sec 125), PWDVA 2005 (Sec 12, 20), HMA 1955 (Sec 24, 25)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 56, 72, 84, 91, 105
* **Material Facts:** Conflicting and prolonged maintenance litigation across multiple forums (Section 125 CrPC, PWDVA Section 12, HMA Section 24) with frequent concealment of income and delayed adjudication.
* **Legal Issue:** What uniform guidelines should be established to streamline maintenance calculation, asset disclosure, and eliminate overlapping awards?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Fair maintenance determination requires mandatory sworn disclosure of all financial assets, liabilities, and lifestyle standards from the date of application.
* **Holding:** **Both parties in all matrimonial maintenance proceedings must mandatorily file a standardized Affidavit of Assets and Liabilities. Maintenance is payable from the date of filing the petition.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Universally followed across all Family Courts and High Courts nationwide.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar — `(2014) 8 SCC 273 / 2014 INSC 492`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 2-Judge Bench (Chandramauli Kr. Prasad, Pinaki Chandra Ghose, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2014-07-02 | **Case Number:** Criminal Appeal No. 1277 of 2014
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MTcwMzc=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MTcwMzc=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `BNSS 2023 (Sec 35) / CrPC 1973 (Sec 41, 41A), BNS 2023 (Sec 85/86) / IPC 1860 (Sec 498A)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 7, 8, 9, 11, 12
* **Material Facts:** Rampant mechanical arrests under Section 498A IPC without satisfying necessity grounds or conducting preliminary investigation.
* **Legal Issue:** Whether police officers can effect automatic arrests in offences punishable with up to seven years imprisonment without judicial scrutiny?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Arrest brings personal humiliation and curtailment of liberty. Police must apply the checklist under Section 41(1)(b) CrPC (Section 35 BNSS) and record reasons before arresting.
* **Holding:** **No automatic arrest in offences punishable with imprisonment up to seven years. Police must issue a notice of appearance under Section 41A CrPC (Section 35(3) BNSS) unless exceptional arrest grounds are documented.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Reiterated and extended to all offences with up to 7 years imprisonment in Satender Kumar Antil (2022).
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh — `(2014) 2 SCC 1 / 2013 INSC 798`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 5-Judge Constitution Bench (P. Sathasivam, C.J., B.S. Chauhan, Ranjana P. Desai, Ranjan Gogoi, S.A. Bobde, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2013-11-12 | **Case Number:** Writ Petition (Criminal) No. 68 of 2008
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MTYwNTM=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MTYwNTM=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `BNSS 2023 (Sec 173) / CrPC 1973 (Sec 154)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 120, 121
* **Material Facts:** Police refusal to register FIR regarding abduction of a minor girl, leading to a reference to Constitution Bench on the mandatory nature of Section 154 CrPC.
* **Legal Issue:** Is a police officer bound to register an FIR upon receiving information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** The word 'shall' in Section 154 CrPC (Section 173 BNSS) is mandatory. Police have no discretion to refuse FIR registration if cognizable offence is disclosed.
* **Holding:** **Registration of FIR is mandatory if information discloses commission of a cognizable offence. Preliminary inquiry is permitted only in specified categories (corruption, medical negligence, matrimonial disputes) and must be concluded in 7-14 days.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Universal constitutional precedent binding on all law enforcement agencies across India.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Jitendra Singh v. State of Madhya Pradesh — `2021 SCC OnLine SC 802 / 2021 INSC 449`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 2-Judge Bench (D.Y. Chandrachud, B.V. Nagarathna, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2021-09-06 | **Case Number:** Civil Appeal No. 5177 of 2021
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzE4MDU=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzE4MDU=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `Karnataka Land Revenue Act 1964 (Sec 128, 129), Madhya Pradesh Land Revenue Code 1959`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 6, 7, 8
* **Material Facts:** Dispute over mutation of agricultural land based on a will, where revenue authorities attempted to adjudicate testamentary genuineness.
* **Legal Issue:** Does mutation in revenue records confer legal title, and can revenue courts adjudicate complex questions of will validity?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Mutation entries in revenue registers (Khata, RTC, Pahani) are solely for fiscal taxation purposes and do not confer, alter, or extinguish legal ownership.
* **Holding:** **Mutation entry neither creates nor extinguishes title. Substantive disputes over title and will validity belong exclusively to Civil Courts, not revenue officers.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Reaffirmed in multiple property decisions confirming Khata/RTC does not equal title.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Imperia Structures Ltd. v. Anil Patni — `(2020) 10 SCC 783 / 2020 INSC 624`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 2-Judge Bench (U.U. Lalit, Vineet Saran, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2020-11-02 | **Case Number:** Civil Appeal No. 3581-3590 of 2020
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzExMjM=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MzExMjM=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `RERA 2016 (Sec 18, 71, 79, 88), Consumer Protection Act 2019 (Sec 35)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 22, 23, 24, 25
* **Material Facts:** Real estate promoter argued that enactment of RERA barred homebuyers from initiating complaint proceedings before Consumer Forums under Consumer Protection Act.
* **Legal Issue:** Whether the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 bars consumer complaint remedies under the Consumer Protection Act?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Section 79 RERA bars civil courts, but Consumer Commissions are specialized quasi-judicial tribunals, not civil courts. Section 88 RERA explicitly provides that RERA is in addition to other laws.
* **Holding:** **Remedies under RERA and the Consumer Protection Act are concurrent and alternative. A homebuyer is entitled to file a consumer complaint for refund and interest due to project delay.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Consistently followed by NCDRC, State Commissions, and Supreme Court.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Danial Latifi v. Union of India — `(2001) 7 SCC 740 / 2001 INSC 486`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 5-Judge Constitution Bench (G.B. Pattanaik, S. Rajendra Babu, D.P. Mohapatra, Doraiswamy Raju, Shivaraj V. Patil, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2001-09-28 | **Case Number:** Writ Petition (Civil) No. 868 of 1986
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=ODcwMA==](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=ODcwMA==)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986 (Sec 3), Constitution of India (Art 14, 21)`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 28, 29, 30, 36
* **Material Facts:** Constitutional challenge to the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986 on the ground that restricting maintenance to iddat period violated Articles 14 and 21.
* **Legal Issue:** Does Section 3(1)(a) of the 1986 Act restrict husband liability for maintenance solely to the iddat period?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Harmonious interpretation dictates that 'fair and reasonable provision' must be made within the iddat period to sustain the divorced woman for her entire life.
* **Holding:** **Upheld the constitutionality of the 1986 Act by interpreting that a Muslim husband must make a reasonable and fair provision within the iddat period that provides maintenance for her lifetime until she remarries.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Foundational constitutional judgment governing Muslim women maintenance rights in India.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab — `(2005) 6 SCC 1 / 2005 INSC 372`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 3-Judge Bench (R.C. Lahoti, C.J., G.P. Mathur, P.K. Balasubramanyan, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2005-08-05 | **Case Number:** Criminal Appeal No. 144-145 of 2004
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=ODg2Mg==](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=ODg2Mg==)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `BNS 2023 (Sec 106(1)) / IPC 1860 (Sec 304A), Consumer Protection Act 2019`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 48, 51, 52, 53
* **Material Facts:** Criminal prosecution initiated against doctor under Section 304A IPC for non-availability of oxygen cylinder in hospital ward.
* **Legal Issue:** What degree of negligence is required to prosecute medical professionals criminally, and what safeguards must precede arrest?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Criminal negligence requires gross rashness or recklessness showing complete disregard for life. Negligence in civil law does not necessarily amount to criminal culpability.
* **Holding:** **To prosecute a doctor under Section 304A IPC / Section 106 BNS, the negligence must be gross. Police must not arrest a doctor without obtaining an opinion from an independent medical expert.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Confirmed in Martin F. D'Souza (2009) and Kusum Sharma (2010).
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`

## Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India — `(2017) 10 SCC 1 / 2017 INSC 652`

* **Court:** Supreme Court of India | **Bench:** 9-Judge Constitution Bench (J.S. Khehar, C.J., J. Chelameswar, S.A. Bobde, R.K. Agrawal, R.F. Nariman, A.M. Sapre, D.Y. Chandrachud, S.K. Kaul, S. Abdul Nazeer, JJ.)
* **Decision Date:** 2017-08-24 | **Case Number:** Writ Petition (Civil) No. 494 of 2012
* **Official Document URL:** [https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MjU1MzQ=](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/view_judgment?id=MjU1MzQ=)
* **Statutory Provisions:** `Constitution of India (Articles 14, 19, 21), DPDP Act 2023, Aadhaar Act 2016`
* **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 262, 310, 311, 320
* **Material Facts:** Challenge to the Aadhaar biometric system referring the foundational question of whether privacy is a constitutionally protected right to a 9-judge bench.
* **Legal Issue:** Is there a fundamental right to privacy guaranteed under the Constitution of India?
* **Ratio Decidendi:** Privacy is an intrinsic facet of human dignity, personal liberty, and autonomy guaranteed under Article 21 and Part III of the Constitution.
* **Holding:** **The 9-judge bench unanimously held that the Right to Privacy is a fundamental right protected under Article 21 of the Constitution. State infringements must satisfy legality, legitimate state aim, and proportionality.**
* **Subsequent Treatment:** Foundational precedent applying to surveillance, digital privacy, and informational autonomy.
* **Applicability Status:** `Controlling Law (Union of India)`
